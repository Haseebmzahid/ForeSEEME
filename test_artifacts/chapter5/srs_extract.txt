Chapter 1: Introduction and Project Overview
This chapter introduces ForeSeeMe as a system and sets the context for the requirements documented in Chapter 2. It covers the background of the project, the problem being addressed, the proposed solution, project objectives, scope, modules, related systems, tools and technologies, and the project contribution.

1.1 Overview of the Project
ForeSeeMe is a Flutter-based mobile application designed to bridge two connected but currently separate problems in public health. On one side, disease surveillance in Pakistan operates reactively outbreaks like Dengue or smog-related respiratory illness only get announced after they have already spread. On the other side, patients with chronic conditions rely on basic alarm-clock apps to manage their medications, and those apps have zero awareness of what is happening in the surrounding environment.
The system addresses both gaps together. A supervised machine learning engine, built in Python using Scikit-learn, is trained on historical public health and environmental data to estimate relative disease risk levels for specific cities. These estimations classified as Low, Medium, or High are then used to trigger personalized push notifications to users who are in high-risk zones and have relevant health vulnerabilities on their profile.
This dual-track approach is what makes ForeSeeMe different from anything currently available. Existing solutions either focus on population-level surveillance for researchers and governments, or they handle individual medication reminders without any environmental context. ForeSeeMe connects those two layers into one platform for everyday users.

1.2 Vision Statement
For individuals with chronic conditions and health-conscious citizens who struggle to maintain medication adherence and lack timely warnings about rising environmental health risks, ForeSeeMe is an AI-assisted disease risk awareness and medication management application that estimates regional disease risk levels and triggers personalized, context-aware alerts to ensure timely medication intake. Unlike fragmented solutions that offer either complex epidemiological dashboards for researchers or static, context-blind alarm clocks for patients, ForeSeeMe integrates predictive public health intelligence directly with personal adherence tracking, empowering users to take proactive, informed actions to protect their health before an outbreak peaks.

1.3 Problem Statement
Despite advances in public health surveillance, disease risk detection in developing regions like Pakistan occurs too late for individuals to take meaningful preventive action. Outbreaks are typically identified only after infection rates have already peaked within a community, leading to increased morbidity that could have been reduced with earlier warnings.
At the individual level, patients with chronic conditions struggle to maintain strict medication adherence. This challenge compounds during outbreak periods when timely medication intake and specific health precautions become critical. Existing apps that try to help with this function as static alarm clocks. They have no awareness of the local environment and send the same reminder regardless of whether the air quality index is normal or dangerously high.
The core problem is that current solutions operate in silos. Public health surveillance systems give macro-level trend data to professionals, and medication apps give micro-level scheduling to individual users, but there is no unified platform that connects macro environmental signals to micro personal health behavior. Vulnerable users remain unaware of rising risks in their own city and fail to take extra precautions that could prevent complications. ForeSeeMe is built specifically to close that gap.

1.4 Problem Solution
ForeSeeMe solves the problem through a three-part approach. First, a Python-based data pipeline aggregates historical health and environmental datasets, preprocesses them, and feeds them into a supervised machine learning model to classify regional disease risk levels. Second, a Flutter mobile application provides users with a clean interface to set up medication schedules, confirm daily doses, and track their adherence history. Third, a backend context logic engine cross-references each user's registered city and health vulnerabilities with the current ML-generated risk estimate and dispatches targeted push notifications via Firebase Cloud Messaging.
Instead of getting a generic reminder, a user registered as an Asthma patient in Lahore will receive a specific alert indicating that smog risk is high in their area and that they should take extra precautions. That is the core value the system delivers. The system also includes a Health Resource Center with verified precautionary content, a geospatial heatmap dashboard for visualizing risk zones, and integration with the Google Places API to surface nearby hospitals during high-risk alert periods.

1.5 Objectives of the Proposed System
The primary objectives of ForeSeeMe are as follows:

PO-1: To estimate regional disease risk levels (Low, Medium, High) using supervised machine learning models trained on historical public health and environmental data such as temperature, humidity, rainfall, and air quality.
PO-2: To improve individual medication adherence through a dedicated scheduling and tracking mobile interface that logs daily doses and provides a personal adherence history.
PO-3: To deliver personalized, context-aware push notifications to vulnerable users when local environmental conditions indicate elevated regional health risk.
PO-4: To visualize active disease risk zones on an interactive geospatial dashboard using color-coded heatmaps to improve public awareness.
PO-5: To provide immediate access to verified self-care guidelines, precautionary tips, emergency contact numbers, and nearby healthcare facilities.

1.6 Scope
The scope of ForeSeeMe is to develop a user-centric, AI-assisted decision-support system that improves individual health preparedness by estimating regional disease risk levels and supporting medication adherence. The system does not perform epidemiological outbreak predictions or medical diagnosis. It classifies relative health risk levels for selected regions using supervised machine learning models trained on historical disease incidence and environmental data.
To ensure technical feasibility and clear environmental correlation, the system focuses on diseases whose occurrence is known to be influenced by environmental conditions specifically dengue fever, influenza-like illness, and smog-related respiratory risks. The platform is implemented as a Flutter-based mobile application. A FastAPI microservice in Python serves the trained ML model through REST endpoints. Firebase handles authentication, the Firestore database, and push notifications. Google Maps SDK renders the risk heatmap, and Google Places API surfaces nearby healthcare facilities during elevated alerts.
ForeSeeMe functions strictly as a health awareness and preparedness tool. It does not interpret individual symptoms, provide clinical recommendations, or replace official public health surveillance systems.

1.6.1 Limitations and Constraints

LI-1:  ForeSeeMe functions solely as a health awareness and decision-support tool. It does not interpret symptoms, provide medical diagnoses, or replace professional healthcare advice.
LI-2:  Risk estimations depend on the availability and update frequency of public health and environmental datasets. Real-time weather API data is used, but official disease incidence data may have reporting delays.
LI-3:  Initial deployment focuses on major metropolitan areas where reliable historical health and environmental data are available. Expanding to additional regions requires consistent datasets for accurate modelling.
LI-4:  The AI engine uses classical supervised machine learning models such as Random Forest. Computationally intensive deep learning models are outside the scope of this implementation.
LI-5:  Medication adherence is recorded based on user confirmation within the application. The system cannot physically verify whether medication was consumed.
LI-6:  The system estimates relative regional health risk levels rather than predicting exact outbreak events or infection counts. Its purpose is to improve preparedness, not provide epidemiological forecasts.
LI-7:  The system focuses on diseases with clear environmental correlations such as dengue, heat-related illness, and smog-related respiratory risk. Diseases without strong environmental indicators are outside the current scope.

1.7 Modules
ForeSeeMe is organized into nine modules. Each module addresses a specific functional area of the system.

Module 1: User Registration & Profile Management
FE-1: Implement secure authentication using Email/Password and Google Sign-In via Firebase.
FE-2: Collect static health factors specifically Current Location (City/Sector) and Chronic Conditions (e.g., Asthma) to create a Risk Profile.
FE-3: Provide options for users to update their location or vulnerability status to maintain accurate alert context.

Module 2: Medicine Reminder & Adherence Tracker
FE-1: Provide a scheduling engine to add medicines with specific dosages, types (Pill/Syrup), and frequencies (e.g., Twice Daily).
FE-2: Trigger local push notifications as smart alarms, functioning even when the app runs in the background.
FE-3: Log daily adherence through a check-in interface allowing users to mark doses as Taken or Skipped.

Module 3: Visualization & Alert Dashboard
FE-1: Integrate the Google Maps SDK to render a heatmap interface with color-coded risk zones (Red = High, Yellow = Medium, Green = Low) based on AI output.
FE-2: Display visual markers or trend lines indicating whether risk in a specific geographic area is Rising, Stable, or Falling.

Module 4: Health Resource Center
FE-1: Host a static library of verified content containing precautions and self-care tips sourced from standard health protocols.
FE-2: Enable emergency quick-actions with one-tap dialing for local services (e.g., Rescue 1122) and links to hospital directories.
FE-3: Use the Google Places API to display nearby hospitals and clinics based on the user's location during high-risk alerts.

Module 5: Environmental & Health Data Aggregation and Preprocessing
FE-1: Run a Python-based pipeline to ingest historical public health data (CSV format) from government or verified open sources such as WHO.
FE-2: Perform automated preprocessing to clean data, handle missing values, and normalize date formats and city names before model input.

Module 6: Disease Risk Estimation & AI/ML Engine
FE-1: Implement supervised machine learning models (e.g., Random Forest or gradient-based classifiers) trained on historical disease incidence and environmental data.
FE-2: Generate a weekly Risk Level classification (Low, Medium, High) for selected geographic regions based on observed historical trends.
FE-3: Evaluate model performance using a train-test split on historical datasets to ensure reliability and consistency.
FE-4: Retrieve current environmental conditions through a weather API and feed these values into the trained model for real-time risk estimation.

Module 7: Personalized Alert System
FE-1: Run a backend context logic engine that cross-references User Location (from Module 1) with current model-estimated regional risk levels (from Module 6).
FE-2: Dispatch targeted notifications via Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) only to relevant users for example, sending smog alerts only to Asthma patients in Lahore.

Module 8: Admin & Analytics Panel
FE-1: Display a system monitoring dashboard showing total active users and overall system status.
FE-2: Provide manual override functionality to update the Risk Level for a city in case of real-time data delays.
FE-3: Include a content management form to upload or edit articles and guidelines in the Health Resource Center.

Module 9: Security, Privacy & Compliance Layer
FE-1: Encrypt user data at rest within Firebase Firestore to ensure data protection.
FE-2: Implement strict Firestore Security Rules to ensure users can only read or write their own medication logs.
FE-3: Enforce a data minimization policy by strictly avoiding the collection of detailed diagnostic symptoms.

2.2 Operating Environment

OE-1:  The ForeSeeMe mobile application runs on Android 8.0 (API level 26) and above. Flutter dependencies require this minimum OS version. iOS support is planned but primary testing targets Android given the dominant market share in Pakistan.
OE-2:  Because the app is built in Flutter, it naturally adapts to different screen sizes without requiring separate designs for each device type. The layout remains stable across budget Android phones and mid-range tablets.
OE-3:  Push notifications, real-time risk data, weather API calls, and Google Places queries all require an active internet connection. A 4G signal or Wi-Fi is sufficient. If the connection drops, previously loaded medication schedules and cached risk data remain visible, but live updates pause until connectivity is restored.
OE-4:  The backend REST API is built with FastAPI and Python, running on a cloud server. Firebase Firestore serves as the primary database. All client-server communication is HTTPS-only.
OE-5:  The trained Scikit-learn model runs in a dedicated FastAPI container, isolated from the main application server. This separation makes model retraining and updates easier without affecting the mobile app's availability.
OE-6:  Notifications on Android are delivered via Firebase Cloud Messaging. Devices without Google Play Services active will not receive FCM alerts.
OE-7:  Initial deployment covers Pakistan, with a primary focus on major metropolitan cities where historical health and environmental data are available. The interface language is English.

2.3 Design and Implementation Constraints

CON-1:  The mobile application must be developed using Flutter and Dart to maintain a single codebase for Android (and future iOS) deployment.
CON-2:  The backend API and ML inference endpoints must be built using Python and FastAPI, served through authenticated REST interfaces.
CON-3:  All data storage, authentication, and push notification services must use Firebase (Firestore + Firebase Auth + FCM).
CON-4:  ML models must be built using Scikit-learn with classical supervised classifiers (e.g., Random Forest). Deep learning models are out of scope.
CON-5:  All client-server communication must use HTTPS. Plain HTTP is not permitted under any condition.
CON-6:  The system must enforce Firestore Security Rules so that users can only read or write their own medication logs and profile data.
CON-7:  The system must not collect detailed diagnostic symptom data from users. Data collection is strictly limited to location and self-reported chronic condition category.
CON-8:  The Geospatial Risk Dashboard must use Google Maps SDK. Alternative mapping libraries are not permitted in this release.

2.5 Requirement Identification Technique

Once the mockup was ready, every interactive element input fields, buttons, toggles, navigation controls, alert panels was examined and translated into a written functional requirement. This ensures that what you see in the design maps directly to what the system is required to do. All requirements derived this way are documented in Section 2.6. The process followed these steps:

Identify the key screens for each of the nine modules.
Assign a Mockup ID to each screen.
Build the screen in Figma with all interactive elements visible.
For each significant element, derive one or more feature-level requirements.
Document each requirement in the format: Feature | FR-ID Statement | Business Rule.

2.7 Non-Functional Requirements

2.7.1 Reliability
NFR-R1:  The FastAPI ML inference service must maintain an uptime of at least 99% per calendar month. Downtime for model retraining or updates must be handled through a blue-green deployment strategy, so the mobile app always has a live inference endpoint.
NFR-R2:  The Firebase Firestore database must retain all medication logs and adherence records without data loss. Weekly automated backups of critical collections must be performed.
NFR-R3:  If the weather API call fails during a scheduled risk estimation cycle, the system must fall back to the most recently stored environmental values and flag the resulting risk estimate as approximate rather than failing silently.

2.7.2 Usability
NFR-U1:  A new user must be able to complete registration, set up their health profile, and add their first medication within 5 minutes of installing the app, without requiring any external documentation beyond what is shown in the onboarding flow.
NFR-U2:  The Risk Dashboard heatmap must be legible on both AMOLED and LCD display types. Color choices for Low, Medium, and High-risk zones must meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast requirements to remain accessible to color-blind users.
NFR-U3:  Push notifications must be worded in plain, non-clinical language. Each notification must include a direct-action link so users do not have to navigate manually to the relevant content.

2.7.3 Performance
NFR-P1:  The app's Home Dashboard must load within 3 seconds on a standard 4G connection. API response time from the FastAPI inference endpoint must not exceed 3 seconds under normal operating conditions.
NFR-P2:  The full preprocessing pipeline must be completed within 30 minutes for a dataset covering one year of city-level health and environmental records. This ensures nightly pipeline runs finish before the morning alert cycle begins.
NFR-P3:  FCM alert dispatch for up to 10,000 concurrent recipients must complete within 5 minutes of the trigger event. Firebase Cloud Messaging's native batching handles this at the infrastructure level.

2.7.4 Security
NFR-S1:  All user passwords must be stored using Firebase Authentication's built-in bcrypt hashing. Plaintext passwords must never be stored or transmitted.
NFR-S2:  The FastAPI ML microservice must only accept requests from the authenticated backend service, not from the mobile client directly. An internal API key or Firebase service account token must be required for all inference calls.
NFR-S3:  The admin panel must require multi-factor authentication. A single-factor login to the admin interface is not permitted.
NFR-S4:  All collected user data must be handled in accordance with Pakistan's applicable data protection guidelines. No user data may be shared with or sold to third parties.

2.7.5 Scalability
NFR-SC1:  The system architecture must support expansion to additional cities by adding new city records to the Firestore database and including those cities in subsequent preprocessing pipeline runs, without requiring changes to the mobile application codebase.
NFR-SC2:  The ML model design must allow for the addition of new disease categories by retraining with updated datasets. Adding a new disease type must not require architectural changes to the FastAPI inference endpoint beyond updating the model file.

3.5.2 Data Security and Privacy Rules
All Firestore collections are protected by server-side Firestore Security Rules that enforce the following constraints:
Users may only read and write documents where userId matches their Firebase Authentication UID.
The riskEstimates collection is readable by all authenticated users but writable only by the FastAPI service through a privileged service account.
All data transmitted between the Flutter app and Firestore is encrypted in transit via HTTPS/TLS.
Sensitive health data (chronicConditions) is stored at rest within Firestore, which provides AES-256 encryption by default.
The system enforces data minimization — only city and condition categories are stored; no detailed diagnostic symptoms are collected.

4.3 Security Techniques
ForeSeeMe implements a multi-layered security architecture to protect sensitive health data, secure authentication flows, and prevent common mobile and web application vulnerabilities.
4.3.1 Authentication Security
Firebase Authentication enforces HTTPS/TLS for all auth token exchanges.
Google OAuth tokens are validated server-side by Firebase before granting access.
Auth tokens (Firebase ID Tokens) are short-lived (1 hour) and automatically refreshed by the SDK.
GoRouter guards intercept every navigation event and redirect unauthenticated users before any protected screen renders.
4.3.2 Data Authorization Security
Firestore Security Rules enforce row-level security: users may only read/write documents where userId == request.auth.uid.
The riskEstimates collection uses a dedicated service account for writes, preventing direct client-side manipulation.
No raw Firebase credentials are stored in the app bundle; all secrets are injected through flutterfire configure and environment variables.
4.3.3 Data Security
All Firestore data is encrypted at rest using AES-256 by Google Cloud infrastructure.
All API calls (Firebase SDK, FastAPI, OpenWeatherMap) are made exclusively over HTTPS.
flutter_secure_storage is used for any locally persisted sensitive values (auth tokens, user ID).

Chapter 5: Testing and Evaluation
 
This chapter documents the testing and evaluation activities conducted to validate the functionality, correctness, and reliability of the ForeSeeMe system. The testing strategy ensures that all system components — the Flutter mobile application, FastAPI ML inference service, Firebase backend, and the alert dispatch engine — operate as specified in the requirements defined in Chapters 1 and 2, and as designed in Chapters 3 and 4.
 
The testing framework covers four complementary methodologies: unit testing, functional testing, business rules testing, and integration testing. Together, these protocols verify that individual functions behave correctly in isolation, user-facing features work end-to-end, business constraints are enforced by the system, and the interfaces between modules remain reliable when components interact.
 
Each test case specifies the test script or scenario, the attribute values used, the expected outcome, the actual result observed, and a pass or fail verdict. Honestly, this chapter became much cleaner once all the modules were well-defined — having precise functional requirements made translating them into test cases a fairly direct process.
 
5.1 Unit Testing
 
Unit testing verifies the smallest independently testable pieces of software — individual functions, utility methods, and isolated service routines — in isolation from other modules. The goal is to confirm that each unit returns correct, predictable outputs for a range of valid and invalid inputs, so that defects can be caught at the earliest stage of development.
 
Unit tests were written and executed for four core function sets: email validation, password handling, the ML risk classification functions, and the medication scheduling utilities. Each set was tested using controlled inputs that cover normal cases, boundary conditions, and error paths.

102: Abstract        
103: The primary rationale for developing  ForeSeeMe  is to bridge the critical disconnect between reactive public health surveillance and individual medication adherence in developing nations. Currently, disease surveillance systems in the region operate in silos, often announcing outbreaks only after infection rates have peaked. On the individual level, mobile health applications function primarily as static alarm clocks, tracking schedules without any awareness of environmental factors. Consequently, vulnerable populations remain unaware of rising regional risks until it is too late to take preventative measures. This lack of contextual awareness leads to poor medication adherence and increased susceptibility to preventable complications during high-risk periods. A significant gap exists in the lack of a unified platform that interprets macro-level disease trends to trigger micro-level personal health interventions. To address this, the proposed project aims to develop an AI-assisted system that analyzes historical health patterns and environmental conditions to estimate regional disease risk levels. It will simultaneously provide a user-friendly mobile interface for rigorous medication scheduling and tracking. Crucially, the system seeks to implement a context-aware alert mechanism that dynamically warns users to take extra precautions when environmental conditions indicate elevated regional health risk. The significance of this project lies in its ability to transform static health data into actionable, personalized preparedness, thereby reducing the burden of infectious diseases through proactive behavior modification.

155: 1.1 Overview of the Project
156: ForeSeeMe  is a Flutter-based mobile application designed to bridge two connected but currently separate problems in public health. On one side, disease surveillance in Pakistan operates reactively outbreaks like Dengue or smog-related respiratory illness only get announced after they have already spread. On the other side, patients with chronic conditions rely on basic alarm-clock apps to manage their medications, and those apps have zero awareness of what is happening in the surrounding environment.
157: The system addresses both gaps together. A supervised machine learning engine, built in Python using Scikit-learn, is trained on historical public health and environmental data to estimate relative disease risk levels for specific cities. These estimations classified as Low, Medium, or High are then used to trigger personalized push notifications to users who are in high-risk zones and have relevant health vulnerabilities on their profile.
158: This dual-track approach is what makes  ForeSeeMe  different from anything currently available. Existing solutions either focus on population-level surveillance for researchers and governments, or they handle individual medication reminders without any environmental context.  ForeSeeMe  connects those two layers into one platform for everyday users.
159: 
160: 1.2 Vision Statement
161: For individuals with chronic conditions and health-conscious citizens who struggle to maintain medication adherence and lack timely warnings about rising environmental health risks,  ForeSeeMe  is an AI-assisted disease risk awareness and medication management application that estimates regional disease risk levels and triggers personalized, context-aware alerts to ensure timely medication intake. Unlike fragmented solutions that offer either complex epidemiological dashboards for researchers or static, context-blind alarm clocks for patients,  ForeSeeMe  integrates predictive public health intelligence directly with personal adherence tracking, empowering users to take proactive, informed actions to protect their health before an outbreak peaks.
162: 
163: 1.3 Problem Statement
164: Despite advances in public health surveillance, disease risk detection in developing regions like Pakistan occurs too late for individuals to take meaningful preventive action. Outbreaks are typically identified only after infection rates have already peaked within a community, leading to increased morbidity that could have been reduced with earlier warnings.
165: At the individual level, patients with chronic conditions struggle to maintain strict medication adherence. This challenge compounds during outbreak periods when timely medication intake and specific health precautions become critical. Existing apps that try to help with this function as static alarm clocks. They have no awareness of the local environment and send the same reminder regardless of whether the air quality index is normal or dangerously high.
166: The core problem is that current solutions operate in silos. Public health surveillance systems give macro-level trend data to professionals, and medication apps give micro-level scheduling to individual users, but there is no unified platform that connects macro environmental signals to micro personal health behavior. Vulnerable users remain unaware of rising risks in their own city and fail to take extra precautions that could prevent complications.  ForeSeeMe  is built specifically to close that gap.
167: 
168: 1.4 Problem Solution
169: ForeSeeMe  solves the problem through a three-part approach. First, a Python-based data pipeline aggregates historical health and environmental datasets, preprocesses them, and feeds them into a supervised machine learning model to classify regional disease risk levels. Second, a Flutter mobile application provides users with a clean interface to set up medication schedules, confirm daily doses, and track their adherence history. Third, a backend context logic engine cross-references each user's registered city and health vulnerabilities with the current ML-generated risk estimate and dispatches targeted push notifications via Firebase Cloud Messaging.
170: Instead of getting a generic reminder, a user registered as an Asthma patient in Lahore will receive a specific alert indicating that smog risk is high in their area and that they should take extra precautions. That is the core value the system delivers. The system also includes a Health Resource Center with verified precautionary content, a geospatial heatmap dashboard for visualizing risk zones, and integration with the Google Places API to surface nearby hospitals during high-risk alert periods.
171: 
172: 1.5 Objectives of the Proposed System
173: The primary objectives of  ForeSeeMe  are as follows:
174: 
175: PO-1: To estimate regional disease risk levels (Low, Medium, High) using supervised machine learning models trained on historical public health and environmental data such as temperature, humidity, rainfall, and air quality.
176: PO-2: To improve individual medication adherence through a dedicated scheduling and tracking mobile interface that logs daily doses and provides a personal adherence history.
177: PO-3: To deliver personalized, context-aware push notifications to vulnerable users when local environmental conditions indicate elevated regional health risk.
178: PO-4: To visualize active disease risk zones on an interactive geospatial dashboard using color-coded heatmaps to improve public awareness.
179: PO-5: To provide immediate access to verified self-care guidelines, precautionary tips, emergency contact numbers, and nearby healthcare facilities.
180: 
181: 1.6 Scope
182: The scope of  ForeSeeMe  is to develop a user-centric, AI-assisted decision-support system that improves individual health preparedness by estimating regional disease risk levels and supporting medication adherence. The system does not perform epidemiological outbreak predictions or medical diagnosis. It classifies relative health risk levels for selected regions using supervised machine learning models trained on historical disease incidence and environmental data.
183: To ensure technical feasibility and clear environmental correlation, the system focuses on diseases whose occurrence is known to be influenced by environmental conditions specifically dengue fever, influenza-like  illness, and smog-related respiratory risks. The platform is implemented as a Flutter-based mobile application. A  FastAPI  microservice in Python serves the trained ML model through REST endpoints. Firebase handles authentication, the  Firestore  database, and push notifications. Google Maps SDK renders the risk heatmap, and Google Places API surfaces nearby healthcare facilities during elevated alerts.
184: ForeSeeMe  functions strictly as a health awareness and preparedness tool. It does not interpret individual symptoms, provide clinical recommendations, or replace official public health surveillance systems.
185: 
186: 1.6.1 Limitations and Constraints
187: 
188: LI-1:  ForeSeeMe  functions solely as a health awareness and decision-support tool. It does not interpret symptoms, provide medical diagnoses, or replace professional healthcare advice.
189: LI-2:  Risk estimations depend on the availability and update frequency of public health and environmental datasets. Real-time weather API data is used, but official disease incidence data may have  reporting delays .
190: LI-3:  Initial deployment focuses on major metropolitan areas where reliable historical health and environmental data are available. Expanding to additional regions requires consistent datasets for accurate modelling.
191: LI-4:  The AI engine uses classical supervised machine learning models such as Random Forest. Computationally intensive deep learning models are outside the scope of this implementation.
192: LI-5:  Medication adherence is recorded based on user confirmation within the application. The system cannot physically verify whether medication was consumed.
193: LI-6:  The system estimates relative regional health risk levels rather than predicting exact outbreak events or infection counts. Its purpose is to improve preparedness, not provide epidemiological forecasts.
194: LI-7:  The system focuses on diseases with clear environmental correlations such as dengue, heat-related illness, and smog-related respiratory risk. Diseases without strong environmental indicators are outside the current scope.

196: 1.7 Modules
197: ForeSeeMe  is organized into nine modules. Each module addresses a specific functional area of the system.
198: 
199: Module 1: User Registration & Profile Management
200: FE-1: Implement secure authentication using Email/Password and Google Sign-In via Firebase.
201: FE-2: Collect static health factors specifically Current Location (City/Sector) and Chronic Conditions (e.g., Asthma) to create a Risk Profile.
202: FE-3: Provide options for users to update their location or vulnerability status to maintain accurate alert context.
203: 
204: Module 2: Medicine Reminder & Adherence Tracker
205: FE-1: Provide a scheduling engine to add medicines with specific dosages, types (Pill/Syrup), and frequencies (e.g., Twice Daily).
206: FE-2: Trigger local push notifications as smart alarms, functioning even when the app runs in the background.
207: FE-3: Log daily adherence through a check-in interface allowing users to mark doses as Taken or Skipped.
208: 
209: Module 3: Visualization & Alert Dashboard
210: FE-1: Integrate the Google Maps SDK to render a heatmap interface with color-coded risk zones (Red = High, Yellow = Medium, Green = Low) based on AI output.
211: FE-2: Display visual markers or trend lines indicating whether risk in a specific geographic area is Rising, Stable, or Falling.
212: 
213: Module 4: Health Resource Center
214: FE-1: Host a static library of verified content containing precautions and self-care tips sourced from standard health protocols.
215: FE-2: Enable emergency quick-actions with one-tap dialing for local services (e.g., Rescue 1122) and links to hospital directories.
216: FE-3: Use the Google Places API to display nearby hospitals and clinics based on the user's location during high-risk alerts.
217: 
218: Module 5: Environmental & Health Data Aggregation and Preprocessing
219: FE-1: Run a Python-based pipeline to ingest historical public health data (CSV format) from government or verified open sources such as WHO.
220: FE-2: Perform automated preprocessing to clean data, handle missing values, and normalize date formats and city names before model input.
221: 
222: Module 6: Disease Risk Estimation & AI/ML Engine
223: FE-1: Implement supervised machine learning models (e.g., Random Forest or gradient-based classifiers) trained on historical disease incidence and environmental data.
224: FE-2: Generate a weekly Risk Level classification (Low, Medium, High) for selected geographic regions based on observed historical trends.
225: FE-3: Evaluate model performance using a train-test split on historical datasets to ensure reliability and consistency.
226: FE-4: Retrieve current environmental conditions through a weather API and feed these values into the trained model for real-time risk estimation.
227: 
228: Module 7: Personalized Alert System
229: FE-1: Run a backend context logic engine that cross-references User Location (from Module 1) with current model-estimated regional risk levels (from Module 6).
230: FE-2: Dispatch targeted notifications via Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) only to relevant  users  for example, sending smog alerts only to Asthma patients in Lahore.
231: 
232: Module 8: Admin & Analytics Panel
233: FE-1: Display a system monitoring dashboard showing total active users and overall system status.
234: FE-2: Provide manual override functionality to update the Risk Level for a city in case of real-time data delays.
235: FE-3: Include a content management form to upload or edit articles and guidelines in the Health Resource Center.
236: 
237: Module 9: Security, Privacy & Compliance Layer
238: FE-1: Encrypt user data at rest within Firebase  Firestore  to ensure data protection.
239: FE-2: Implement strict  Firestore  Security Rules to ensure users can only read or write their own medication logs.
240: FE-3: Enforce a data minimization policy by strictly avoiding the collection of detailed diagnostic symptoms.

They register an account, set up their health profile by specifying their city and any chronic conditions, and use the app for medication scheduling and daily adherence tracking. 280: 2.2 Operating Environment
281: 
282: OE-1:  The  ForeSeeMe  mobile application runs on Android 8.0 (API level 26) and above. Flutter dependencies require this minimum OS version. iOS support is planned but primary testing targets Android given the dominant market share in Pakistan.
283: OE-2:  Because the app is built in Flutter, it naturally adapts to different screen sizes without requiring separate designs for each device type. The layout remains stable across budget Android phones and mid-range tablets.
284: OE-3:  Push notifications, real-time risk data, weather API calls, and Google Places queries all require an active internet connection. A 4G signal or Wi-Fi is sufficient. If the connection drops, previously loaded  medication schedules and cached risk data remain visible, but live updates pause until connectivity is restored.
285: OE-4:  The backend REST API is built with  FastAPI  and Python, running on a cloud server. Firebase  Firestore  serves as the primary database. All client-server communication is HTTPS-only.
286: OE-5:  The trained Scikit-learn model runs in a dedicated  FastAPI  container, isolated from the main application server. This separation makes model retraining and updates easier without affecting the mobile app's availability.
287: OE-6:  Notifications on Android are delivered via Firebase Cloud Messaging. Devices without Google Play Services active will not receive FCM alerts.
288: OE-7:  Initial deployment covers Pakistan, with a primary focus on major metropolitan cities where historical health and environmental data are available. The interface language is English.

290: 2.3 Design and Implementation Constraints
291: 
292: CON-1:  The mobile application must be developed using Flutter and Dart to maintain a single codebase for Android (and future iOS) deployment.
293: CON-2:  The backend API and ML inference endpoints must be built using Python and  FastAPI , served through authenticated REST interfaces.
294: CON-3:  All data storage, authentication, and push notification services must use Firebase ( Firestore  + Firebase Auth + FCM).
295: CON-4:  ML models must be built using Scikit-learn with classical supervised classifiers (e.g., Random Forest). Deep learning models are out of scope.
296: CON-5:  All client-server communication must use HTTPS. Plain HTTP is not permitted under any condition.
297: CON-6:  The system must enforce  Firestore  Security Rules so that users can only read or write their own medication logs and profile data.
298: CON-7:  The system must not collect detailed diagnostic symptom data from users. Data collection is strictly limited to location and self-reported chronic condition category.
299: CON-8:  The Geospatial Risk Dashboard must use Google Maps SDK. Alternative mapping libraries are not permitted in this release.

301: 2.4 Use Case Diagram
302: Three use case diagrams are presented below, one per primary actor. Each diagram defines the scope of that actor's interactions with  ForeSeeMe , including inclusion and extension relationships. These diagrams serve as the basis for the functional requirements in Section 2.6.
303: Actor: General User (Patient / Health-Conscious Citizen)

339: 2.5 Requirement Identification Technique
340: Two complementary techniques were used to ensure complete and consistent requirement  coverage  one for everything the user interacts with directly, and one for all background system behaviors.
341: 
342: Technique 1: Mockup-Based Requirement Analysis
343: For each core screen in  ForeSeeMe , a Figma mockup was built first. Each screen was assigned a Mockup ID starting from M1. Once the mockup was ready, every interactive element input fields, buttons, toggles, navigation controls, alert panels was examined and translated into a written functional requirement. This ensures that what you see in the design maps  directly  to what the system is required to do. All requirements derived this way are documented in Section 2.6. The process followed these steps:
344: 
345: Identify the key screens for each of the nine modules.
346: Assign a Mockup ID to each screen.
347: Build the screen in Figma with all interactive elements visible.
348: For each significant element, derive one or more feature-level requirements.
349: Document each requirement in the format: Feature | FR-ID Statement | Business Rule.

573: 3.1 System Architecture Overview
574: 3.1.1 Purpose
575: The architecture of  ForeSeeMe  is designed to achieve four primary goals:  (1) strict separation of concerns between UI, business logic, and data access;  (2) scalability to support the future integration of a dedicated Python/ FastAPI  machine learning microservice; 
576: (3) testability by keeping the domain layer free of Flutter framework dependencies; and  (4) security by routing all external data access through abstract repository interfaces backed by Firebase.
577: The architecture ensures that the Flutter presentation layer never communicates directly with Firebase or any external API. All data flows exclusively through  Riverpod  providers and repository abstractions, making the system both maintainable and auditable.

875: 3.5.2 Data Security and Privacy Rules
876: All  Firestore  collections are protected by server-side  Firestore  Security Rules that enforce the following constraints:
877: Users may only read and write documents where  userId  matches their Firebase Authentication UID.
878: The  riskEstimates  collection is readable by all authenticated users but writable only by the  FastAPI  service through a privileged service account.

892: 4.1.2 Algorithm
893: The following algorithms constitute the core intelligence and data processing logic of  ForeSeeMe . Each algorithm is described with its input, output, and step-by-step pseudocode representation.

919: 4.3 Security Techniques
920: ForeSeeMe  implements a multi-layered security architecture to protect sensitive health data, secure authentication flows, and prevent common mobile and web application vulnerabilities.
921: 4.3.1 Authentication Security
922: Firebase Authentication enforces HTTPS/TLS for all auth token exchanges.
923: Google OAuth tokens are validated server-side by Firebase before granting access.
924: Auth tokens (Firebase ID Tokens) are short-lived (1 hour) and automatically refreshed by the SDK.
925: GoRouter  guards intercept every navigation event and redirect unauthenticated users before any protected screen renders.
926: 4.3.2 Data Authorization Security
927: Firestore  Security Rules enforce row-level security: users may only read/write documents where  userId  ==  request.auth.uid .
928: The  riskEstimates  collection uses a dedicated service account for writes, preventing direct client-side manipulation.
929: No raw Firebase credentials are stored in the app bundle; all secrets are injected through  flutterfire  configure and environment variables.
930: 4.3.3 Data Security
931: All  Firestore  data is encrypted at rest using AES-256 by Google Cloud infrastructure.
932: All API calls (Firebase SDK,  FastAPI ,  OpenWeatherMap ) are made exclusively over HTTPS.
933: flutter_secure_storage  is used for any locally persisted sensitive values (auth tokens, user ID).
934: 4.3.4 Mobile Application Security

Business rules testing validated the domain-specific constraints that govern medication data, adherence logging, and user data isolation. Integration testing confirmed that all modules — authentication, medication management, daily check-in, local notifications, and theme persistence — work correctly when wired together as a complete system.

1239: 5.3 Business Rules Testing
1240: Business rules testing validates the domain-specific constraints that govern how  ForeSeeMe  handles healthcare data. These rules are distinct from general functional  behaviour  — they define the system's correctness boundaries. For example, it is a business rule that an adherence log's document ID must be a deterministic composite of  medicationId , date, and  reminderTime ; it is a business rule that an unauthenticated request must never reach a user's  Firestore  sub-collection; and it is a business rule that a medication with  isActive  = false must never generate a dose card in the daily check-in schedule.

1342: All 162 test cases passed with no failures recorded during the testing phase. The results confirm that the  ForeSeeMe  authentication system, medication management module, daily check-in workflow, local notification service, theme management system, and navigation guard are all operating correctly at the current implementation milestone.
1343: 
1344: Several defects that were discovered and resolved during development are worth noting. Silent  Firestore  save failures were caused  by exception  swallowing inside the  Riverpod   notifier and  were fixed by introducing explicit error propagation through the repository chain. Medication dose cards were initially disappearing from the Check-In screen due to an overly  strict   createdAt  timestamp filter; this was corrected by simplifying the active medication scheduling predicate. Android notification delays were addressed by enabling  exactAllowWhileIdle  scheduling and requesting the exact alarm permission at  runtime on Android 13. Google Sign-In failures were resolved by correcting the SHA-1 key registered with the Firebase project. These fixes were all verified through the test cases documented in this chapter.
